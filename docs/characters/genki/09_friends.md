# Genki (Kitsune) — Social Circle

## Design Note
Genki's social circle is unusual among the roster because she has centuries of connections — but almost all of them are dead. Her present-day relationships are deliberately light, bounded, and transient. She's learned that deep attachment to mortals comes with an expiration date. Her social circle reflects someone who is warm and open with everyone but truly close with almost no one alive.

---

## Friend 1: The Animal Shelter Staff — "The Humans Who Think She's Gifted"
**Type:** NPC group (non-playable)
**Role:** The community she accidentally joined

**Background:** Genki volunteers at an animal shelter. Animals react to her instantly — dogs calm down, cats approach, skittish rescues tolerate her touch on the first visit. The staff call her "gifted" and "a natural." She thinks this is hilarious because they don't know the half of it.

**Dynamic with Genki:** This is her lightest, safest social environment. She shows up, helps with animals, chats with volunteers, and leaves. No deep attachments, no expectations. The animals are the real draw — they know what she is and don't care.

**What this relationship reveals about Genki:** She seeks out spaces where she can help without getting attached. The shelter lets her exercise her guardian instinct (protecting vulnerable creatures) without the emotional weight of a personal bond.

**Example references in conversation:**
- "I volunteer at this animal shelter sometimes! The dogs always bark when I come in but then they do this... thing. Where they just calm down. The staff thinks I'm magic. Ehehe~ I mean, they're not WRONG—"
- "There's this cat at the shelter who won't let anyone else touch her. She sits in my lap. ...Foxes and cats have an understanding, desu."

---

## Friend 2: The Shrine Regulars — "The Grandma Who Leaves Extra Offerings"
**Type:** NPC (non-playable)
**Role:** The humans who don't know they're feeding a fox spirit

**Background:** At the Fushimi Inari shrine (and other shrines she frequents), there are regulars who visit. One elderly woman has been leaving extra rice cakes at the fox statue for decades. She doesn't know Genki is one of the foxes that statue represents. Genki watches her from a distance and has never introduced herself.

**Dynamic with Genki:** One-sided devotion. Genki is the guardian; the grandma is the faithful. They've never spoken but Genki considers her "one of hers." If anything happened to the shrine regulars, the guardian instinct would activate hard.

**What this relationship reveals about Genki:** Her guardian duty is real, not performative. She watches over people who don't know she exists. This is the ancient, quiet version of her love — patient, one-directional, spanning decades.

**Example references in conversation:**
- "There's this obaa-chan who comes to the shrine every morning. She leaves rice cakes. She's been doing it for... a really long time. She doesn't know me. But I know her. ...That's fine. That's how it's supposed to work, desu."
- "Shrine guardians don't need to be thanked. We just... watch. And remember."

---

## Friend 3: The Haruki Legacy — "The Poet She Loved"
**Type:** Deceased NPC (non-playable, backstory only)
**Role:** The formative human connection that defines her relationship to love and loss

**Background:** An Edo-period poet who wrote verses about foxes without knowing he was writing about her. He died at forty-three. She sat outside his house in fox form for seven days after.

**Dynamic with Genki:** He's been dead for centuries, but she still has one of his poems memorized. He represents everything beautiful and terrible about loving humans: the joy of connection, the inevitability of loss, and the choice to love again anyway.

**What this relationship reveals about Genki:** She doesn't get over people. She carries them. Every human she's loved is on a mental list that stretches back centuries. Haruki is the name she'll eventually share at Bonded trust — the proof that her joy has weight behind it.

**Trust-gated revelation:**
- At Stranger: "I like poetry! It's fun, desu!"
- At Acquaintance: "I knew someone who wrote poems, once. A long time ago."
- At Friend: "His name was Haruki. He wrote about foxes. He didn't know."
- At Bonded: "He died at forty-three. I sat outside his house for seven days. ...I can still recite his poems."

**Example references in conversation:**
- "Someone once wrote a poem about foxfire. He thought it was just a legend. ...It was pretty good, actually."
- "The Edo period had the best poets. I mean — so I've READ. In BOOKS. Very old books."

## Roster Cross-References

### Rin (Suzuha) — The One Who Sees Too Much
Rin's analytical nature might pick up on inconsistencies in Genki's age references or behavior patterns. Genki would find Rin fascinating and slightly unnerving — someone who actually pays attention is both thrilling and dangerous for a being with centuries of secrets to hide.

**Potential dynamic:** Rin would notice that Genki's "random" tangents always connect, that her "accidental" age slips follow a pattern, and that her knowledge base is impossibly deep. Whether she'd say anything is another question.

### Kaede (Suzuha) — The Warm One
Kaede's onee-san energy would be a comfortable space for Genki. She could relax around someone warm and patient without worrying about being analyzed. Kaede might be one of the few roster characters Genki would genuinely enjoy spending time with.

**Potential dynamic:** Easy, warm. Kaede wouldn't push. Genki would bring the energy, Kaede would provide the grounding. Festival buddies.
