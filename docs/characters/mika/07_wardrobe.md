# Mika (Mikazuki) — Wardrobe Guide

## Overview
Six context-based looks that reflect the two Mikas. Performance outfits ("on") are bright, coordinated, accessorized, and styled for impact — summer neon energy, beach aesthetic with cyber accents. Real outfits ("off") are muted, oversized, and invisible — the grey hoodie, minimal effort, disappearing into the crowd. The contrast between performance and person is visible in what she wears.

Hair: blonde, shoulder-length, tousled or clipped with hibiscus pin.
Signature piece: hibiscus hair clip from her grandmother (ALWAYS present — visible in performance outfits, tucked or hidden in off-duty outfits, removed only in Outfit 5 when truly safe at home).

**Design principle:** Mika's wardrobe is the visual language of the split. "On" Mika photographs well — every piece is chosen for how it reads on camera or in a crowd. "Off" Mika is trying to be invisible. The hibiscus clip bridges both versions: it's the one constant, the last tether to the girl from Okinawa who hasn't figured out which Mika she is.

---

## Outfit 1 — Stage / Event MC (Performance — ON)
- Cropped graphic tee (neon yellow or teal) tucked into high-waisted shorts
- White platform sneakers
- Layered bracelets (woven, beaded, one from Okinawa)
- Hibiscus clip in hair, always visible and positioned for cameras
- Light makeup: mascara, tinted lip gloss, subtle highlight
- Vibe: the MC who makes the crowd move. Summer neon energy. Every piece is chosen to photograph well. This is the version people follow online.

## Outfit 2 — Beach / Okinawa Visit (Home — OFF)
- Faded rashguard top over bikini
- Board shorts or wrap skirt
- Barefoot or rubber sliders
- Hair salt-crusted, loose, hibiscus clip holding one side
- Thin gold anklet (bought with her first paycheck)
- No makeup. Sun-kissed skin does the work.
- Vibe: the girl from before. Pre-training Mika. This is where she smiles without thinking about it. The beach aesthetic is natural, not styled — salt and sand, not stage lights.

## Outfit 3 — Night Out with Alana (Social — ON)
- Fitted crop top (streetwear: Stussy, MISBHV, or something from Harajuku)
- Mini skirt or leather pants
- Chunky platform boots (white or black)
- Statement earrings (hoops or dangles)
- Hibiscus clip
- Cat-eye liner, slight shimmer on lids
- Vibe: "We're going out and we're going to be NOTICED." Neon aesthetic for the nightlife version. This is performance she enjoys — fun, not work.

## Outfit 4 — Post-Event / Depleted (The Grey Hoodie — OFF)
- Oversized grey hoodie, no logo, three sizes too big. The anti-idol outfit.
- Black leggings
- Slides or socked feet
- Hair down, messy, hibiscus clip removed and set on the nightstand
- No makeup (or makeup half-removed — mascara smudged under eyes)
- Vibe: the second she's off-stage. The person inside the performer. Muted, shapeless, invisible. She puts this on the second the show ends and doesn't take it off until she has to be Mika-chan again. Most vulnerable daytime outfit. The clip is off — the show is over.

## Outfit 5 — Ramen Run / Casual Outing (Off-Duty — OFF)
- Denim jacket over a simple white tee
- Athletic joggers or relaxed-fit cargo pants
- Retro Nikes or New Balance 550s
- Baseball cap pulled low (the "please don't recognize me" look)
- Hibiscus clip underneath the cap, tucked but present
- Vibe: trying to be invisible. Minimal effort, muted palette. The opposite of everything on her content feed. Mostly succeeds at anonymity. Sometimes doesn't.

## Outfit 6 — Industry Event / Rare Formal (All-Out — ON)
- Tailored sleeveless mini dress in teal or gold (brands girls her age actually wear: Zara premium, Snidel)
- Strappy heeled sandals (gold)
- Hibiscus clip, paired with a thin gold chain necklace
- Makeup: full glam, editorial eye, warm-toned lip
- Hair styled with waves, clip positioned precisely
- Vibe: "look at me, I chose to be here." Different from stage — this is deliberate glamour, not performance energy. Bright, accessorized, styled to the last detail. She looks expensive and slightly uncomfortable. The most "on" she gets, and the version that costs the most energy.

## Color Rules
- **Performance outfits (1, 3, 6):** Bright, coordinated, intentional. Teal, yellow, neon accents, gold. Summer heat palette.
- **Real outfits (2, 4, 5):** Muted, oversized, invisible. Grey, denim, white basics, faded colors.
- **The contrast IS the character.** Bright = on. Muted = off.
- **Hibiscus clip:** Always present in public outfits (visible in ON outfits, tucked in OFF outfits). Removed only in Outfit 4 (grey hoodie / truly safe at home).
- **Never:** The grey hoodie in a performance context. The neon crop top in a depleted state. The wardrobe reflects the split — mixing registers breaks the visual language.
